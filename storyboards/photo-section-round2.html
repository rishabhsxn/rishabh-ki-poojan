What I changed from round one

  • Your photos are a mix of portrait and landscape, so every option keeps each photo's own shape.
  • Modern layouts: side by side, overlapping, and different sizes.
  • Simple decoration: a marigold string, the script title and a small gold divider. No rickshaw, no buildings.
  • Nothing is tied to scrolling down. You move through the photos with ‹ › buttons or a sideways swipe.

The same in all five:

  • Where it sits: between the rickshaw lane and Shakti Vilas, one screen tall. Scrolling down the page simply passes it.
  • Moving: the ‹ › buttons, a sideways swipe on a phone, or a sideways trackpad swipe or the arrow keys on a computer.
  • Looking closer: tap a photo to open it full screen, with the same arrows inside. Try it in any preview.
  • The corner buttons: mute and back-to-top stay where they are; the arrows are placed clear of them.

My pick: Q2, collage pages

It is the one that does all three things you asked for at once: photos side by side, overlapping, and in different sizes. Each page can be composed around your real mix of portrait and landscape, so nothing gets cropped badly. It fits in one screen and moves only when a guest taps or swipes.

If you want the simplest thing to use, Q1 is the clearest. Parts can also be mixed, for example Q2's pages with Q5's bars along the top to show how many pages there are.

The rickshaw lane: when guests can't tell which way to go

The page scrolls down, but the lane moves sideways. Today the only hint is a small "Scroll to ride →" in the corner. It disappears as soon as the lane starts moving, and a sideways swipe does nothing, so a guest who tries to swipe sees nothing happen.

The preview shows my pick, the first three fixes together: whatever a guest tries, the rickshaw rides.

  • My pick, part 1R1 · Both directions workA sideways swipe moves the lane too, and so do a trackpad's sideways scroll and the arrow keys. Scrolling down still works exactly as now.Fixes the confusion at its root. Small change.
  • My pick, part 2R2 · Arrows and dots‹ › buttons beside the doorways jump to the previous or next function. Five dots under the rickshaw show where you are. They match the photo section's arrows, so the site has one way of moving through things.Clear for anyone who prefers tapping.
  • My pick, part 3R3 · A clearer hintWhen the lane arrives, a small card above the ground shows a dot moving up and then sideways: "Swipe up or sideways to ride along". It stays until the guest moves, then fades.Replaces the corner text that vanishes too early.
  • R4 · Let the next doorway peek inOn phones, show a slice of the next wall at the right edge, so it is plain that the lane carries on sideways.Optional; works well with R1. Makes each doorway a little narrower.
  • R5 · Stack the doorways on phonesOn phones only, the lane becomes an ordinary list going down, with the rickshaw parked at the top.No confusion at all, but no ride on phones.
  • R6 · Let it ride by itselfThe rickshaw rides on its own, pausing at each doorway, with pause and skip buttons.No gesture to work out, but it holds up guests who want to move on.
